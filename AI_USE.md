# How ARIADNE uses AI

NASA Space Apps 2026 calls AI tools "Super Intelligence (SI)" and asks teams to show clearly where and how they were used, in descriptive text and metadata. This file is that disclosure. It says plainly who did what, including where the idea came from.

## The short version

- **The idea was generated by AI.** At the team's request, Claude (Anthropic's assistant, on claude.ai) generated the ARIADNE concept, the project plan ([docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md)) and the build brief ([PROMPT.md](PROMPT.md)) from the challenge statement on November 8, 2026 (UTC).
- **Two facts, both stated:** the idea was SI-generated, and that happened before the 2026 SI rule was published. The 2026 Project Submission Guide, published on November 13, 2026, says SI should not be used to come up with the idea of how to solve the challenge.
- **Claude Code** (Anthropic's AI coding assistant) wrote the code, ran the pipeline, the tests and the checks, and drafted the documents.
- **The team** chose the challenge, directed the work, approved or rejected each decision, got clearance from the Local Lead, and is responsible for the submission.
- **No AI-generated images, video or audio, with one exception:** the ARIADNE mark (the small symbol beside the name and the browser-tab icon), which Claude Code drew as SVG code from two straight strokes and a dot at the team's request, with no image generator. Every other picture is real mission imagery with its credit, or a chart or map drawn from data.

## Who did what

### Claude (claude.ai)

- **Generated the concept and plan** on November 8, 2026 (UTC), at the team's request, from the challenge statement: the "thread home" that every route must keep, rehearsal in 3D past real Curiosity photos, a visor that shows at most four things, a cuff tablet, a printed cuff card, and the May 2024 storm replay. The plan was committed as [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md) (commit `92cbcca`, 21:02 Pacific on November 7, which is November 8 in UTC).
- **Wrote the build brief** ([PROMPT.md](PROMPT.md), committed at kickoff on November 8) and the source list it starts from.
- **Researched sources** for the plan and the brief.
- **Reviewed each phase report** and **proposed most of the decisions** the team then approved or rejected.

### Claude Code

- Wrote the Python pipeline (fetching and cropping NASA and USGS data, the derived layers, the route engine with OR-Tools, the Time Machine) and the static web app (React, deck.gl and three.js).
- Wrote the tests for each phase's acceptance criteria and ran them (343 pipeline tests, 100 unit tests and 15 browser tests at submission).
- Checked every data source before the code depended on it, fetching it and recording what it found in [docs/SOURCES_LOG.md](docs/SOURCES_LOG.md), and flagged sources that had moved or could not be confirmed.
- Ran the data processing and reported the results, including the ones that missed their targets, and diagnosed problems (for example the DEM seams behind the rover-check false alarms, and a timeline that showed the wrong comm shadows after the relay change).
- Drafted the documents: README, this file, PROGRESS, CREDITS, the submission text, the video script and the slide outline.
- Redesigned the user interface on November 15, 2026, at the team's request and to its brief (an app shell, a dark design system, floating map panels, route cards, the visor drawer and compass tape, validation cards, the About page, the first-visit intro and the judge tour), changing no data, number, label or check.
- Drew the ARIADNE mark on November 15 and 16, 2026, at the team's request: a peak that is both the A of the name and Mount Sharp, with a dot for the walker on it, in the app's cyan-to-violet accent. It is SVG code (web/public/favicon.svg and web/src/brand/), written by hand from two straight strokes and a dot; no image generator was used and no third-party artwork was copied. The app's footer says so beside it.

- Approved or rejected each decision. The decisions, most proposed by Claude, are recorded with dates in [docs/PROGRESS.md](docs/PROGRESS.md); wherever the repository says "team decision", it means a decision approved by the team, most of them proposed by Claude.

## Decisions approved by the team, most proposed by Claude

These shaped the result; each is dated in docs/PROGRESS.md.

- **Site and calibration:** the Mount Sharp boxwork as the flagship site; the Murray Buttes RAD measurement (Ehresmann et al. 2021) to calibrate the shielding layer; the four boxwork drill holes as science anchors.
- **A failed check, reported openly:** when the rover-walkability check failed (79.8% against a 95% target), slope at each traveller's footprint scale on physical grounds, one re-run only, the failed result kept beside the new one, a held-out test at Murray Buttes (99.8%, pass), then no further re-runs and a conservative planner.
- **What a plan must be:** a 6-hour walk with a 30-minute reserve; whole fracture, wall and hollow transects; presets that differ by safety settings.
- **The rover's role:** a mobile radio relay, then the only shelter, with storm mode choosing the lowest modeled dose.
- **What to show:** the price-of-safety line, the note that slope no-go is conservative in boxwork terrain, and the station steps (Look, Observe, Describe, Document), which the team took from NASA's 2023-24 SUITS mission description.
- **Final sprint:** the storm replay first, then the submission documents; Murray Buttes as the second site built by the same pipeline; the ridge map left "not validated" without hand labels; the Python pipeline run under WSL rather than changing any Windows security setting.

## Where to see it in the project

- **Source code:** every source file starts with `Written with AI assistance (Claude Code). See AI_USE.md.` A test fails if any file lacks it.
- **Commits:** every commit from the kickoff on ends with `AI-assisted: Claude Code`, checked in CI. The two earlier commits were made by the team without that line: the initial commit, and the project plan, whose content Claude generated.
- **Data:** the packaged site's manifest carries an `ai_assistance` field, and every generated file records the pipeline version that made it.
- **The app:** the footer says the concept was generated with Claude and the software written with Claude Code.
- **The video:** its end card says the same. The video itself is screen recordings of the app and real NASA imagery; nothing in it is AI-generated.

## Rules Claude Code worked under

From [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md), read at the start of every session:

- **No invented data.** Every value comes from a cited source, a calculation on it, a documented model or a labeled placeholder.
- **Every number is labeled** measured, derived, modeled or placeholder, with its source on hover.
- **Verify before depending** on a source, and ask the team when a source fails.
- **No NASA logos, flags, insignia or mission identifiers** as graphics, and no AI-generated media apart from the ARIADNE mark described above.
- **Ask the team** before paid services, unusual licenses, deleting cached data, or changing the site.
